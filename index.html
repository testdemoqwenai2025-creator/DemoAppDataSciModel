The Kimball four-step process
Every dimensional model in this solution follows the classic Kimball lifecycle, with stakeholder engagement woven through each step.
1. Select the business process

Identify the operational activities the organisation wants to measure — for example sales, shipments, claims or registrations. Each becomes a fact table grain.

2. Declare the grain

State precisely what one row in the fact table represents. The grain must be atomic and consistent; it drives every subsequent design decision.

3. Identify the dimensions

List the descriptors that give context to the measures — who, what, where, when and why. These become conformed dimensions where possible.

4. Identify the facts

Define the numeric measures that make sense at the declared grain, distinguishing additive, semi-additive and non-additive measures for the ETL and BI teams.

New: Distributed Platform Extension

Evolving toward a distributed scientific data platform

The Kimball warehouse is the foundation. The distributed platform extension evolves it toward a microservices architecture serving exceptionally complex scientific workloads — a hypothetical multi-domain platform spanning climate, genomics and astrophysics, with provenance as a first-class dimension and the bus matrix as the service map.

The deeper insight

The bus matrix is the service map. Each row becomes a service that owns a business process; each conformed dimension becomes a master-data service. The warehouse stops being a monolith and becomes a read-side projection of the event streams emitted by autonomous services.

Event sourcing ≡ SCD Type 2 at scale. An event-sourced service is just an SCD2 dimension materialised from its event log. The warehouse becomes a projection, not a separate truth — and reproducibility becomes a SQL query, not an archaeological dig.
