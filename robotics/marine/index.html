Marine robotics: blackout, biofouling, pressure nonlinearity

AUVs and ASVs operate in the harshest communication environment on Earth — underwater. Acoustic modems give 9.6 kbps at 2-second latency, and thermoclines can black them out entirely. Biofouling drifts sensors over months. Pressure-depth conversion becomes nonlinear at hadal zone depths. This page covers the edge cases and corner cases.

The core insight

An AUV is a time capsule. It collects data for months, surfaces, and syncs. The warehouse must treat the sync as a batch import with a retroactive timeline — every reading gets a timestamp from months ago but arrives today. The dimensional answer: the mission segment is the black box. It carries the current model, sensor health and comms status that were in effect. When something goes wrong, the segment tells you why.

Edge cases

Acoustic Communication Blackout
Problem

An AUV (autonomous underwater vehicle) at 3000 m depth communicates via acoustic modem at 9.6 kbps with 2-second latency. At 4000 m, a thermocline reflects the acoustic signal — total blackout. The AUV must continue its mission autonomously and surface to re-establish contact. The mission service does not know whether the AUV is still alive.

Impact

The AUV is blackout for 6 hours. The mission service marks it as "lost". A recovery ship is dispatched ($50K/day). The AUV surfaces on schedule with all data — but the warehouse has already marked the mission as failed, and the recovery ship was wasted.

Dimensional solution

Add a dim_comm_link dimension (acoustic-low, acoustic-high, satellite, none) and a fact_comm_episode table that tracks every blackout episode with start, end, depth, and cause (thermocline, distance, interference). The mission service uses a state machine: ACTIVE → BLACKOUT (after 5 min no comms) → PRESUMED-LOST (after 24h). The warehouse tracks the state transitions, not just the current state. When the AUV surfaces and syncs its log, the blackout episode is retroactively closed.

Biofouling Sensor Drift Over Months
Problem

An AUV on a 6-month mission accumulates biofouling (algae, barnacles) on its CTD (conductivity-temperature-depth) sensor. The conductivity reading drifts 2% per month. The data quality team does not notice because the drift is gradual. After 6 months, all salinity measurements are 12% off — the entire mission's oceanographic data is compromised.

Impact

6 months of oceanographic data is published with a systematic 12% salinity bias. Climate models that consume the data produce wrong ocean circulation predictions. The error is discovered 2 years later by a cross-validation against satellite altimetry. The paper must be retracted.

Dimensional solution

Add a dim_sensor_health dimension (clean, light-fouling, moderate-fouling, heavy-fouling, cleaned). The fact_sensor_reading table carries a sensor_health_key and a drift_estimate (computed by comparing to a reference sensor or a pre-mission calibration). When drift exceeds 5%, a sensor.drift_detected event is emitted. The warehouse can answer "which readings were affected by biofouling?" as a query on sensor_health_key, and the data can be re-calibrated retroactively.

Pressure-Depth Nonlinearity at Hadal Zone
Problem

An AUV diving to the Mariana Trench (10,935 m) uses the standard UNESCO pressure-to-depth conversion, which assumes constant gravity and a linear density profile. At hadal zone depths (>6000 m), water compressibility changes the density profile significantly. The depth calculation is off by 50 m at 10,000 m. The AUV's altitude-above-seafloor reading is wrong, and it collides with a seamount.

Impact

The AUV collides with the seafloor at 10,000 m. The pressure housing cracks. The vehicle is lost with all data. The warehouse stored the depth as 10,000 m (using the linear formula) — the actual depth was 9,950 m. The collision investigation cannot determine the true altitude.

Dimensional solution

Add a dim_depth_equation dimension (UNESCO-linear, UNESCO-nonlinear, TEOS-10, custom). Every fact_pose_estimate carries a depth_equation_key and both the linear and nonlinear depth estimates. For hadal zone missions, the warehouse stores the nonlinear depth (TEOS-10) as the authoritative value, with the linear depth as a reference. The altitude-above-seafloor is computed from the nonlinear depth minus the bathymetric DEM (digital elevation model), not from the linear depth.

Corner case: Current-Based Mission Re-Planning with Biofouling Drift

An AUV on month 4 of a 6-month mission is surveying a hydrothermal vent field at 2500 m. A deep current shifts by 30° (unusual eddy). The AUV must re-plan its survey grid to compensate. Simultaneously, its CTD sensor has moderate biofouling (5% drift), and the acoustic comms are degraded by the eddy's thermocline effect.

Navigation

The current has shifted 30°; the AUV must re-plan its survey grid, but the current model is based on the (now-stale) pre-mission forecast

Sensor

The CTD drift is 5%, meaning the salinity data (which feeds the density calculation) is wrong, which feeds the depth calculation

Comms

The eddy's thermocline blocks acoustic comms; the AUV cannot receive the updated current model from the surface

Failure mode

The AUV re-plans using the stale current model and the drifted salinity data. The depth calculation is off by 8 m. The AUV flies into the hydrothermal vent chimney (hard obstacle). The vehicle is lost. The warehouse has the stale plan, the drifted sensor data, and the blackout episode — but cannot correlate them because they are in three separate fact tables with no shared key.

Solution

The AUV carries an edge logger that tags every decision with a mission_segment_key (a new segment starts whenever the current model, sensor health, or comms status changes). The warehouse stores fact_mission_segment with start_time, end_time, current_model_version, sensor_health_key, and comm_episode_key. When the AUV is lost, the last segment before loss is the "black box" — it contains the current model, sensor health and comms status that were in effect. Reproducibility is a query: "show me the mission segment that was active at the time of loss, with all its dimensional context".

Dimensional model (SQL DDL)
-- Marine robotics dimensional model
-- Handles acoustic blackout, biofouling drift, pressure-depth nonlinearity

CREATE TABLE dim_comm_link (
  comm_key     INT PRIMARY KEY,
  link_type    VARCHAR(20),  -- acoustic-low, acoustic-high, satellite, none
  bandwidth_bps INT,
  latency_ms   INT,
  max_depth_m  INT           -- maximum operational depth
);

CREATE TABLE dim_depth_zone (
  zone_key     INT PRIMARY KEY,
  zone_name    VARCHAR(30),  -- epipelagic, mesopelagic, bathypelagic, abyssal, hadal
  depth_min_m  INT,
  depth_max_m  INT,
  pressure_range_dbar VARCHAR(20)
);

CREATE TABLE dim_sensor_health (
  health_key   INT PRIMARY KEY,
  health_state VARCHAR(20),  -- clean, light-fouling, moderate, heavy, cleaned
  drift_pct    DECIMAL(5,2), -- estimated drift
  action       VARCHAR(20)   -- none, flag, recalibrate, replace
);

CREATE TABLE dim_depth_equation (
  equation_key INT PRIMARY KEY,
  equation     VARCHAR(30),  -- UNESCO-linear, UNESCO-nonlinear, TEOS-10
  is_hadal_safe BOOLEAN,     -- true for TEOS-10 at hadal depths
  precision_tier VARCHAR(20)
);

-- Fact: communication episode (blackout tracking)
CREATE TABLE fact_comm_episode (
  episode_key  BIGINT PRIMARY KEY,
  auv_key      INT,
  comm_key     INT REFERENCES dim_comm_link,
  start_time   TIMESTAMP,
  end_time     TIMESTAMP,
  duration_s   INT,
  depth_m      DECIMAL(8,2),
  cause        VARCHAR(30)   -- thermocline, distance, interference, eddy
);

-- Fact: sensor reading (with health + drift)
CREATE TABLE fact_sensor_reading (
  reading_key  BIGINT PRIMARY KEY,
  auv_key      INT,
  health_key   INT REFERENCES dim_sensor_health,
  equation_key INT REFERENCES dim_depth_equation,
  zone_key     INT REFERENCES dim_depth_zone,
  timestamp    TIMESTAMP,
  temperature_c DECIMAL(6,3),
  salinity_psu  DECIMAL(6,3),
  pressure_dbar DECIMAL(10,2),
  depth_m       DECIMAL(8,2),
  depth_nonlinear_m DECIMAL(8,2),  -- TEOS-10 depth
  drift_estimate DECIMAL(5,2)      -- estimated drift at this reading
);

-- Fact: mission segment (the "black box")
CREATE TABLE fact_mission_segment (
  segment_key  BIGINT PRIMARY KEY,
  auv_key      INT,
  mission_key  INT,
  start_time   TIMESTAMP,
  end_time     TIMESTAMP,
  current_model_version VARCHAR(20),
  health_key   INT,
  comm_episode_key BIGINT,
  is_final     BOOLEAN  -- true if this was the segment before loss
);
AUV mission analytics notebook
Run in the JupyterLite REPL to simulate a 7-day AUV mission and compute blackout, biofouling drift, current shift and pressure-depth nonlinearity analytics.
# AUV Mission Analytics — JupyterLite Notebook
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

np.random.seed(42)

# Simulate an AUV 6-month mission, 1 Hz sampling
# 6 months × 30 days × 86400 s = 15.5M rows — use lazy eval
# For demo: 7 days × 86400 = 604K rows

n = 7 * 86400
data = {
    "timestamp": pd.date_range("2026-01-01", periods=n, freq="1s"),
    "depth_m": np.maximum(0, 2500 + np.sin(np.arange(n)/3600) * 200 + np.random.randn(n)*5),
    "temperature_c": 2 + np.random.randn(n) * 0.5,
    "salinity_psu": 35 + np.cumsum(np.random.randn(n) * 0.001),  # drifting!
    "pressure_dbar": 2500 * 1.025 * 9.81 / 10000 + np.random.randn(n) * 2,
    "comm_link": np.random.choice(["acoustic", "satellite", "none"], n, p=[0.7, 0.1, 0.2]),
    "sensor_health": np.random.choice(["clean", "light", "moderate"], n, p=[0.6, 0.3, 0.1]),
    "current_speed_ms": np.maximum(0, 0.3 + np.random.randn(n) * 0.15),
    "current_dir_deg": (180 + np.cumsum(np.random.randn(n) * 0.1)) % 360,
}
df = pd.DataFrame(data)

print("=== AUV Mission Summary (7 days) ===")
print(f"Duration: 7 days @ 1 Hz = {len(df):,} rows")
print(f"Depth range: {df['depth_m'].min():.0f} - {df['depth_m'].max():.0f} m")
print()

print("=== Communication Link ===")
print(df["comm_link"].value_counts(normalize=True).round(3))
blackout_pct = (df["comm_link"] == "none").mean() * 100
print(f"Blackout time: {blackout_pct:.1f}%")
print()

print("=== Biofouling Drift Analysis ===")
print(df["sensor_health"].value_counts(normalize=True).round(3))
# Simulate drift: salinity should be ~35, check cumulative drift
drift = df["salinity_psu"].iloc[-1] - df["salinity_psu"].iloc[0]
print(f"Salinity drift over 7 days: {drift:.3f} PSU")
print(f"Estimated drift rate: {drift/7:.3f} PSU/day")
print()

print("=== Current Analysis ===")
print(f"Mean current speed: {df['current_speed_ms'].mean():.2f} m/s")
print(f"Current direction range: {df['current_dir_deg'].min():.0f}° - {df['current_dir_deg'].max():.0f}°")
# Detect current shifts (>30° change in 1 hour)
dir_change = df["current_dir_deg"].diff(3600).abs()
dir_change = dir_change.where(dir_change < 180, 360 - dir_change)
shifts = dir_change[dir_change > 30]
print(f"Current shifts >30° in 1h: {len(shifts)}")
print()

print("=== Pressure-Depth Nonlinearity Check ===")
# UNESCO linear vs nonlinear at 2500m
p_linear = df["depth_m"] * 1.025 * 9.81 / 10000
p_actual = df["pressure_dbar"]
error = ((p_actual - p_linear) / p_actual * 100)
print(f"Mean pressure error (linear vs actual): {error.mean():.3f}%")
print(f"Max pressure error: {error.abs().max():.3f}%")

Microservices decomposition
  • auv-state-service — owns dim_robot, fact_pose_estimate. Uses TEOS-10 for hadal depths.
  • comm-service — owns dim_comm_link, fact_comm_episode. Tracks blackout episodes.
  • sensor-health-service — owns dim_sensor_health. Computes drift estimates; emits sensor.drift_detected.
  • mission-segment-service — owns fact_mission_segment. The "black box" — carries all dimensional context.
