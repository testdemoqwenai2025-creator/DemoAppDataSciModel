Enterprise Architecture

The Kimball enterprise bus matrix

The bus matrix is the architectural roadmap of an enterprise dimensional warehouse. It plots business processes (rows) against conformed dimensions (columns). Each intersection marked with a dot represents a dimension that participates in that business process — and therefore a conformed dimension that must be designed consistently across the matrix.

Why the bus matrix matters

The bus matrix is the single most important artefact in a Kimball programme. It is the contract between business stakeholders and the data team: it shows which business processes the warehouse will support, and which conformed dimensions each of those processes needs. By designing each conformed dimension once and reusing it across the matrix, the organisation gains integrated, drill-across analytics without resorting to a monolithic up-front build.

Delivery proceeds row by row. Each completed row becomes a data mart. Because the dimensions are conformed across rows, those data marts integrate naturally — the enterprise warehouse emerges as the union of the matrix, not as a separate top-down effort.

Reading the matrix

Rows are business processes — each becomes a fact table at a declared grain. Read a row left-to-right to see which dimensions that fact table will reference.

Columns are conformed dimensions. Read a column top-to-bottom to see every fact table that depends on that dimension. A column with many filled cells is a high-leverage dimension — design it carefully, once.

Diagonals reveal integration opportunities: where two rows share multiple dimensions, drill-across reporting becomes possible between their data marts.

Delivery sequencing

The matrix is delivered incrementally. We typically sequence by priority, but adjust based on which conformed dimensions are ready. A P1 row whose dimensions are not yet conformed is blocked until those dimensions land.

In the sample above, Date, Product, Store and Geography are the highest-leverage dimensions — they appear in five or more rows — so they are conformed first.

Conformance: the rule that makes the matrix work

A dimension is conformed when it means the same thing in every fact table that references it. Conformance is achieved through shared surrogate keys, shared attribute labels, shared hierarchies and a shared business glossary definition. Two fact tables that share a conformed dimension can be queried together using that dimension — this is the drill-across capability that distinguishes an enterprise warehouse from a collection of siloed data marts.

In this solution, every dimension that appears in more than one row of the bus matrix is treated as conformed by default. Conformance is enforced through the master data management process, the ETL surrogate key assignment, and the semantic layer definition — all three must agree.
